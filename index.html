Everything here is a section you can open. Start at 01 and you will have a beat playing in about a minute. The rest is there when you want it.

01Your first beat
  1. Press if you skipped the power-on screen.
  2. Press , then pad 9, then SOUND again. You are now on a drum slot.
  3. Press . The pads are now the sixteen steps of the bar.
  4. Tap steps 1, 5, 9, 13. Each tap plays the sound as it writes it, so you hear the beat assemble.
  5. Press WRITE again, then .

That is the whole instrument in five steps. Everything below is a variation on it.

02The one idea

The 16 pads are two things at once. In play mode they trigger sounds. In WRITE mode the same pads are the 16 steps of the bar.

Every mode button works the same way: press it, the pads mean something new, press it again to come back. SOUND makes them slots. PATTERN makes them patterns. Hold FX makes them effects.

pads sounds WRITE steps SOUND slots PATTERN bars
03Choose a sound
  1. Press . Lit LEDs show which slots have something in them.
  2. Press a pad to pick the active slot. 1–8 are melodic — one sample played at sixteen pitches. 9–16 are drum — sixteen separate sounds on sixteen pads.
  3. Press SOUND again to leave.

While SOUND is held the pads wear a dot. amber means that slot has steps in the bar you are on, so it is part of what you are hearing right now. green means the slot is chopped — its sixteen pads are your slices, so think before you write over it. The dots appear only in SOUND, because everywhere else the same pads mean something different.

04Load your own samples

The library lives under the screen (on a phone it takes the screen over).

  1. Press SOUND then a pad to choose the slot you are filling. The library header says loads into SOUND n.
  2. Pick a pack, press ▶ to preview, then click the sample. It loads instantly.
  3. Press SOUND to exit. The pads and the keyboard now play your sample.

slot… on a row targets a specific slot without changing the selected one. The Slots tab shows the whole 1–16 map at once.

05Record a sample
  1. Choose the target slot first: SOUND + a pad.
  2. In the library bar pick 🎙 microphone or 🖥 browser tab audio, then press ● record — or the orange RECORD key on the unit.
  3. For tab audio, choose the tab playing the sound in the share dialog and tick Share tab audio. Press ■ stop when done; it stops itself at 20s.
  4. The clip lands on the selected slot and in the ★ my recordings pack. download .wav keeps it; drop that file in samples/ to make it permanent.

Capturing the Spotify or YouTube desktop apps is not possible from a browser on macOS. Use their web players in a tab, or route audio through a loopback device and pick it as the microphone.

06Trim & chop

Press TRIM to open the waveform. Drag the two handles to set where the sample starts and ends. Tap a pad while it is open to hear the result. This is how you get rid of the silence at the front of a recording, which is usually why a pad feels late.

  • set BPM reads the length of what you trimmed and sets the tempo so the loop fits the bar.
  • ✂ chop cuts the sample into sixteen pieces and lays them across a drum slot's sixteen pads, so a two-bar break becomes sixteen playable hits.
  • on transients cuts where the sound actually hits rather than at even intervals — better for breaks, worse for pads and drones.
  • pitch instead lays the sample across sixteen pads as sixteen pitches.

Chops go onto drum slots 9–16. A melodic slot holds one sample by design, so there is nowhere to put sixteen pieces. A chopped slot gets the green dot.

How long should the sample be?

Work backwards from the slice, not the sample. A slice wants to be somewhere around a quarter to a half second. Shorter than about 0.15s it clicks with no tail; longer than about 0.8s it runs into the next step and turns to mud.

  • chop x16 → sample 5 to 6 seconds, which is two bars at 85–95 BPM. This is the classic break chop: each pad is an eighth note, so you get whole kick-and-snare gestures with their tails.
  • chop x8 → sample 2.5 to 3 seconds, one bar at the same tempo.
  • Melodic or vocal chops want longer — half a second to a second a slice, so 8 to 16 seconds of source.

Sampling 10 or 15 seconds and chopping it x16 gives you slices near a second each. They overlap the steps after them and the loop smears. Trim to the phrase you actually want first.

Getting a chop into a pattern

This is the part that catches everyone. In WRITE the pads are steps, not slices, so every step you tap gets whichever slice is currently selected. Tap out eight steps and you have written the same slice eight times — one note, over and over. That is the instrument working as designed, not a fault.

Three ways to get different slices onto different steps:

  1. LIVE — the one to use. Press LIVE and play the pads in time. Each hit records the slice you actually struck, at the step you struck it. Re-arranging a break is a performance, not data entry.
  2. Hold the step in WRITE to latch it, then drag slider 1. The readout shows note 1–16 — that is which slice that one step plays. Good for fixing a single hit.
  3. The slow way: leave WRITE, tap the slice you want (in perform mode a pad tap also selects it), go back into WRITE, place just that slice's steps. Repeat per slice.
07Locking settings to one step

In WRITE mode, hold a step for a moment instead of tapping it. That step latches, and the sliders now change only that step rather than the whole part. This is the single most useful thing in the instrument and the least obvious.

  • Accents. Latch a hi-hat on the beat and push its volume up, pull the off-beats down. Same notes, suddenly a pulse.
  • Micro-timing. With a step latched, the on-screen timing ◀▶ pushes it a few milliseconds early or late. A snare 10ms late is the whole "behind the beat" feel.
  • Fills. Latch the last few steps and lock a higher pitch or a shorter length onto them.

The song view counts how many steps carry locks, so you can see at a glance whether a part is programmed or played.

08Patterns & chaining
  1. Hold — press it to toggle the hold.
  2. Press a pad to select a pattern. Press more pads to append them to the chain.
  3. Press PATTERN again to exit.

To copy a pattern: in pattern-select mode press WRITE, choose the source, then choose an empty slot.

A chain of 1 1 1 3   2 2 2 3 is eight bars that breathe: a main loop, a variation, and a turnaround at the end of each half.

09Tempo, swing & volume
  1. Press , then drag the two sliders. The label says which is swing and which is tempo. A mouse wheel over a slider works too.
  2. Double-tap BPM to cycle the presets: 80 / 120 / 140.
  3. On this screen the pads preview the selected sound at each pitch, so you can judge how it sits while you drag.

Master volume has its own always-on MASTER slider in the control strip.

Swing 15–22% for hip-hop, 0% for techno.

10The keyboard

The keys tab under the screen is a real instrument, not a sample being retuned: an electric piano, a pad, a pluck and a bass, all built out of maths rather than audio files, so they add nothing to the download.

The pads are laid out in a scale and show their real note names, so there is no wrong note to avoid. If you picked a key in UTIL it uses that; otherwise C minor pentatonic, five notes that sit well over almost any beat.

  • in time holds each note until the next sixteenth, so a slightly late finger still lands with the drums. It changes when you play, never what.
  • rec writes what you play into the current pattern. The keyboard has its own track, kept alongside the sixteen sample slots rather than taking one, so it costs you no sounds. It appears as the ♪ row in the song view.
  • clear wipes the keyboard track on this pattern only.

The XY pad's SYNTH mode plays the same notes with your finger: left to right picks the note, up and down goes dark to bright. With rec armed, sliding writes the note you land on each time you cross onto a new one.

11The XY pad & stacking

Each of the pad's three modes can be locked on its own. Set a filter you like, press LOCK, switch to SPACE — the filter keeps running underneath while you play with the echo. They stack rather than replace.

  • An orange dot on a mode means it is still running in the background.
  • Faint circles show where each locked mode was left.
  • The readout names what else is on, e.g. play a lead +tone +space.
  • Hold LOCK to clear every lock at once.
12The 16 punch-in effects

Same set, same order as the hardware. Hold FX and a pad, or use the fx tab. They are momentary: they last exactly as long as you hold, and they never change your pattern.

Most of them are not audio effects at all. They bend the sequencer — shorten the loop, reverse the step pointer, stutter it, pin the chain so the bar repeats.

  1. loop 16 — loops the whole bar and stops the chain moving on
  2. loop 12 — loops the first 12 steps, so the bar limps
  3. loop short — loops 4 steps around where you are
  4. loop shorter — loops 2 steps, near machine-gun
  5. unison — thickens the beat into one wide layer
  6. unison low — unison with the low end pushed
  7. octave up — whole beat an octave up
  8. octave down — whole beat an octave down
  9. stutter 4 — holds each step across a group of 4
  10. stutter 3 — groups of 3, three-against-four
  11. scratch — pitch-bends the held step like a hand on a record
  12. scratch fast — the same, twice the speed
  13. 6/8 quantize — forces a triplet shuffle onto the grid
  14. retrigger — re-fires from step 1 every 4 steps
  15. reversed — plays the bar backwards
  16. no effect — bypass. The safe pad: it kills whatever was running

Transitions: 4 to build, 15 to flip the bar, 16 to drop back clean.

13Recording the effects

The effects have their own lane, the same as the keyboard. In the fx tab press rec (or arm the device's LIVE REC, which does both), press PLAY, and hold an effect. Every step the playhead crosses while you hold is written down, so a two-bar reverse comes back as a two-bar reverse rather than a blip.

On playback the lane engages and releases the effect for you. Touching a pad always overrides it. clear wipes the lane on this pattern.

14The MIX tab

Mute and solo, plus the pattern chain, on one screen — the "what is my arrangement actually doing" view.

  • Slots with nothing on them in this pattern are dimmed, so you can see what you are actually muting.
  • The chain row shows your arrangement with the playing link lit. Tap a link to remove it.
  • The add row appends a pattern to the end.
  • Every chain edit is a separate undo step.
15The song view

Tap song in the top bar of the screen. Every sound that has something on it gets a row of sixteen cells, filled where it hits and hollow where it rests. The outlined column is the playhead.

Along the top: the pattern, the tempo, the scale, how many parts are playing, how many hits in total, how full the bar is, how many steps carry locks, anything muted or soloed, and how long a bar and a full chain last in seconds. Each row names the sample in that slot. Muted parts are drawn hollow, a soloed one underlined. Along the bottom: the chain.

Tap scene to go back to the animation.

16Drum kits

The keys tab has a drum kit row: 808, 909 and lo-fi. Built out of maths rather than files, so they add nothing to the download, and they load onto the selected drum slot's sixteen pads as real audio — so you can trim them, chop them and lock them per step exactly like a sample.

Kick, snare, hats, clap, toms, rim and cowbell, in that order across the pads.

17Projects & saving

Your work saves itself. The small dot in the corner of the screen blinks green every time the session is written, which is after every edit. You never have to remember to save.

The PROJ button opens the project browser. A project is a complete snapshot: patterns, chain, tempo, swing, volume, which samples are in which slots, the keyboard track and the effects lane.

  • + new starts a genuinely empty project, not the shipped demo beats.
  • save as keeps a new copy under the name in the box; save updates the one you are in.
  • Tap a project to select it. Its actions appear underneath: open, rename, move to a folder, copy, export, delete.
  • Folders group projects. The chips along the top filter by folder; + folder makes a new one.
  • export writes a real .po33.json file; import reads one back. export all backs up everything at once.

Projects live in the browser's own storage. It survives updates, but clearing site data erases it. Exporting is how you get a beat off this device.

18UTIL — key, scale & the rest

The UTIL button opens the settings screen.

  • scale & key sets what the melodic slots and the keyboard play. Pick a scale and a root note and every melodic pad falls inside it. oct +/- shifts the whole layout up or down.
  • LFO sweeps the filter on its own, with rate and depth. Good for a pad that moves without you touching it.
  • metronome gives you a click to play against.
  • motion rec records slider movements into the pattern. Arm it, press PLAY, ride the filter, and the sweep plays back every loop.
  • haptics turns the tick you feel on every pad on or off.
  • clear wipes this pattern, just the locks, or everything — all of it undoable.

The default "16-pad classic" layout is the original hardware's fixed note order and is not a scale at all — the pads do not even run in pitch order. A melody written across it comes out closer to noise than a tune. Pick a real scale before you write melodies.

19Undo & redo

Two arrows in the top bar. ↶ undo steps back, ↷ redo puts it back. Both are also on the keyboard: ⌘Z and ⌘⇧Z (Ctrl on Windows).

It covers steps, chain edits, clears and the keyboard track. The screen tells you what it just undid, so you are never guessing. A greyed-out arrow means there is nothing left in that direction.

A run of fast edits collapses into one undo, so tapping in sixteen hats and changing your mind is one press, not sixteen.

20Three beats to copy

Boom-bap — 90 BPM, swing 18%

  • Kick on 1, 7, 11.
  • Snare on 5, 13 — same drum slot, different pad. Each step remembers its own pad, so one slot holds the whole kit.
  • Hats on every odd step.

House — 124 BPM, swing 0%

  • Kick on 1, 5, 9, 13. Open hat on 3, 7, 11, 15. Clap on 5, 13.
  • Bass on 1, 9 with a higher note on 7, 15.

Trap — 140 BPM, half-time

  • Kick on 1, 11. Snare on 9 only — that single snare is what makes it half-time.
  • Hats on every step, then latch a run and nudge alternate hats for rolls.

Clear the pattern first: UTIL → clear pattern.

21Played, not programmed

Three things separate a beat that sounds made from one that sounds typed in, and none of them are the notes.

  • Dynamics. A hi-hat line where every tick is the same volume reads as a machine. Accent the beat, drop the off-beats back. Add ghost notes — very quiet snare taps between the main ones that fill space without adding a part.
  • Swing. Straight sixteenths are a grid. Delaying every second one turns it into a groove. Boom-bap sits around 56%.
  • Push and drag. A snare landing 11ms late is the "behind the beat" feel that defines 90s hip-hop. Old school has none of it, which is exactly why it sounds stiffer and more upfront.

All three are step locks — see 07.

And one thing about the notes: the bass follows the kick. Bass and kick written separately fight each other in the low end and turn to mud. Landing them on the same steps is most of what makes a beat sound finished. Write the melody an octave above the bass for the same reason.

Less is more. When a loop feels wrong the fix is almost always removing a part, not adding one.

24Tap the little guy

Tap him where he stands on the screen and he climbs out of it. Once he is loose he wanders the actual interface — the real edges of the pads, the mode row, the sliders and the perform tabs are the ledges he stands on. He is walking on the instrument rather than on a picture of one.

He takes his time. He strolls, stops to look around, turns at the edge of a pad rather than marching off it (though he misjudges it now and then, and falls), and occasionally goes to stand under a blinking red LED to watch it. Press a pad and he looks over.

Press PLAY and he dances — turns to face you and bounces on every step, harder on the downbeat, with a side-step each half bar.

He can never get in your way: his layer ignores taps entirely, so every pad underneath still works. Tap him again and he goes home. He touches nothing — no audio, no patterns, nothing saved.
