HOW TO PLAY ME!

Get started

  1. Click if you skipped the power-on screen.
  2. Press to start the sequencer. Press it again to stop.
  3. When playback is stopped, press any button to play the currently selected sound live.

Keyboard

  • Number pads 1–16: keys 1234 / QWER / ASDF / ZXCV
  • Return = PLAY  ·  5 = SOUND  ·  6 = PATTERN  ·  7 = BPM  ·  8 = FX  ·  9 = WRITE

♪ Let's make a song

The one idea

The 16 pads are two things at once: in play mode they trigger sounds, in WRITE mode the same pads are the 16 steps of the bar.

Beat #1 — boom-bap (beginner)

  1. Clear the pattern first (info drawer / PO33.clearPattern()).
  2. BPM ~90, swing ~18%.
  3. Kick: SOUND → pad 9 → SOUND. Audition pads, land on a deep thump. WRITE → steps 1, 7, 11 → WRITE.
  4. Snare (same slot 9): find a crack. WRITE → steps 5, 13 → WRITE. Each step remembers its own pad, so one drum slot holds the whole kit.
  5. Hats: a tick sound, WRITE → steps 1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15 → WRITE.
  6. PLAY. Done.

Make it groove

  • Accent: in WRITE, hold a step to latch it, nudge slider 2 up (TONE mode) — louder hit. Off-beat hats = bounce. Pull some down for ghost notes.
  • Micro-timing: latch the snare, use the on-screen timing ◀▶ (or scroll a step) to push it 6–12ms late for a laid-back feel.
  • Swing 15–22% for hip-hop, 0% for techno. Less is more — add space, not layers.

Two more beats

  • House ~124, swing 0: kick 1,5,9,13; open hat 3,7,11,15; clap 5,13; bass note on 1,9 + a higher note on 7,15 (write each pitch separately).
  • Trap ~140 half-time: kick 1,11; snare 9; hats every step, then latch a run and nudge alternate hats for rolls.

From beat to song

  1. Pattern 1 = the main loop. Get it right.
  2. Pattern 2 = a variation. Copy pattern 1 (PATTERN mode → WRITE → source → empty slot), then strip it back — drop the kick, or mute a part (hold its pad in SOUND).
  3. Pattern 3 = a fill / turnaround: busy hats, a snare roll, or a tape-stop at the end.
  4. Chain: hold PATTERN and tap e.g. 1 1 1 3   2 2 2 3 for an 8-bar loop that breathes.

Full arrangement: intro (drums, 4 bars) → groove (all, 8) → break (melody+hats, 4) → groove (8) → outro (LFO filter down, 4).

Advanced

  • Fills: latch the last few steps and lock a higher pitch or shorter TRIM length.
  • Motion-record a filter sweep: utilities → arm motion rec, PLAY, ride the FILTER slider.
  • Transitions: hold FX → 4 loop shorter to build, 15 reversed to flip the bar, 16 to drop back clean.
  • Chop a break: record 1–2 bars, ✂ chop x16, tick lay out + match tempo, re-order the pads.

Choose a sound

  1. Press . Lit LEDs show which slots have a sound.
  2. Press a to pick the active slot. 1–8 are melodic (each NUMBER = a different note); 9–16 are drum (each NUMBER = a different pad).
  3. Press again to leave sound-select.

Load your own samples (Library, bottom of screen)

  1. Press then a to choose which slot you're filling. The Library says "loads into SOUND n".
  2. Pick a pack, press ▶ to preview, then click the sample — it loads onto that slot instantly.
  3. Press to exit. Now the pads (and keys) play your sample. Use it live, or WRITE it into a pattern.
  4. slot… on a row lets you target a specific slot without changing the selected one. The Slots tab shows the whole 1–16 map.

Record a sample

  1. Choose the target slot first ( + ).
  2. In the Library bar pick 🎙 microphone or 🖥 browser tab audio, then press ● record (or tap the orange pad on the unit).
  3. For tab audio: in the share dialog choose the Chrome tab playing the sound (YouTube / Spotify web) and tick “Share tab audio”. Play the part you want; press ■ stop (auto-stops at 20s).
  4. The clip lands on the selected slot and in the ★ my recordings pack. download .wav to keep it — drop it in samples/ to make it permanent.
  5. Capturing the Spotify/YouTube desktop apps isn't possible from a browser on macOS — use their web players in a tab, or route audio through a loopback device (BlackHole / Loopback) and pick it as the microphone.

Build a pattern

  1. Press to enter edit mode.
  2. Press a button (a step 1–16) to add the current sound to that step.
  3. Press again to return to performance mode, then pick a NUMBER to set the pitch / sample used when writing.

Chain patterns into a song

  1. Hold (press it to toggle the hold).
  2. Press a to select a pattern; press more numbers to append them to the chain.
  3. Press again to exit.

Tempo, swing & volume

  1. Press , then drag the two sliders (label shows what they do): swing and tempo. Mouse wheel over a slider also works. While you're dragging either one, a click plays live so you can hear the pace change as you set it.
  2. Double-tap to cycle the preset tempos (80 / 120 / 140).
  3. On this screen the NUMBER pads preview the selected sound at each pad's pitch, so you can tap through them while you drag the tempo and judge how the sound sits at that speed. Master volume has its own always-on MASTER slider in the control strip — drag that instead.

On your phone (install as an app)

  1. Open the site in a browser (see the URL your host gives you).
  2. iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen.   Android: menu → Install app / Add to Home screen.
  3. Launch it from the home-screen icon — runs full-screen and works offline after the first load.
  4. Everything is touch: tap pads, drag sliders. Tab-audio recording is desktop-Chrome only; mic recording works on Android and recent iOS Safari.

Record & effects

  1. While playing, hold for 3 seconds to live-record what you play into the pattern.
  2. Tap to switch what the two sliders do (TONE / FILTER / TRIM). Hold it instead and the 16 pads become punch-in effects.
  3. In Pattern-select mode press to copy a pattern, then choose an empty slot to paste it.

The keyboard, and recording it

The keys tab under the screen is a real instrument, not a sample being retuned: an electric piano, a pad, a pluck and a bass, all built out of maths rather than audio files, so they add nothing to the download. Tap the one you want — they are all on show.

The pads are laid out in a scale (a chosen set of notes that sound good together) and show their real note names, so there is no wrong note to avoid. If you have picked a key and scale in UTIL it uses that; otherwise it uses C minor pentatonic, five notes that sit well over almost any beat.

  • in time — holds each note until the next sixteenth of the beat, so a slightly late finger still lands with the drums. It changes when you play, never what.
  • rec — writes what you play into the pattern you are on. The keyboard has its own track, kept alongside the sixteen sample slots rather than taking one of them, so recording onto it costs you no sounds. It shows up as the ♪ row in the song view.
  • clear — wipes the keyboard track on this pattern only.

The XY pad's SYNTH mode plays the same notes with your finger: left-to-right picks the note, up-and-down goes from dark to bright.

It records too. With rec armed, sliding your finger writes the note you land on each time you cross onto a new one — hold still and it writes once, slide across and it writes the run you played. A slide is smooth and a pattern only has sixteen slots, so what comes back is the shape of what you played rather than the exact glide.

Stacking the XY effects

Each of the pad's three modes can be locked on its own. Set a filter you like, press LOCK, then switch to SPACE — the filter keeps running underneath while you play with the echo. They stack rather than replacing each other, so you can build a sound up layer by layer.

  • An orange dot on a mode means it is still running in the background.
  • Faint circles on the pad show where each locked mode was left.
  • The readout names what else is on, e.g. play a lead +tone +space.
  • Hold LOCK for a moment to clear every lock at once.

BUILD — triple-press TRIM

A beat is made of a few jobs: drums to keep time, bass to hold the bottom, melody to carry the tune. BUILD tells you which of those this pattern is missing and offers to fill them.

  • build a whole beat is the big one. It writes four bars — an intro (drums alone), the main bar, a breakdown with the kick dropped so the track can breathe, and a turnaround with a fill at the end — then chains them into eight bars and sets the tempo. Press it and press PLAY.
  • fill what's missing works on this bar only and leaves your work alone — it adds just the jobs that are empty.
  • replace what's here wipes the bar first, for when you want to start it over.
  • style sets the rhythm, the tempo, the swing and the scale: old school (102–108, straight — Sugarhill and Run-DMC), boom bap (86–94, swung, snare behind the beat), trap (138–144), house (122–126) and lo-fi (72–80, heavily swung).
  • Every press rolls a new beat inside the rules of the style, so roll a different one gives you something else that's still correct.
  • If a slot is already busy it uses the next free one rather than giving up, and the panel lists exactly what it did and where.
  • Any build is one undo away — the button is right there.

What BUILD is actually doing

It is not listening to your music. It reads your pattern data — which of the 16 slots have steps on them, and roughly how high or low the notes sit — and decides from that which of the three jobs (drums, bass, melody) nothing is covering yet. Then it writes a part for each missing job, using the rules of whichever style is selected, into the first slot that is free and has a sound loaded.

  • It leaves chopped slots alone. If you chopped a sample onto a slot, the arrangement of pieces across its pads is your work and the whole point of chopping — writing a generic kick pattern over it would throw that away. The panel lists any slots it is skipping for this reason.
  • Press it as many times as you like. Every press rolls new numbers inside the style's rules, and repeated presses now walk forward to genuinely unused patterns instead of landing on the same ones twice.
  • It only ever touches slots with a sound loaded — by default 1–4 and 9–12. Load samples into the others and it will use them too.
  • Undo covers a whole build in one press.

What makes the beats sound played rather than programmed

Three things, and none of them are the notes:

  • Dynamics. A hi-hat line where every tick is the same volume reads as a machine. The beat gets accented and the off-beats drop back, so the same notes have a pulse. Boom bap and lo-fi also get ghost notes — very quiet snare taps between the main ones that fill space without adding another part.
  • Swing. Straight sixteenths are a grid. Delaying every second one turns it into a groove — boom bap sits around 56%.
  • Push and drag. A snare landing 11ms late is the "behind the beat" feel that defines 90s hip-hop. Old school gets none of it, which is exactly why it sounds stiffer and more upfront.

And one thing about the notes: the bass follows the kick. Bass and kick written separately fight each other in the low end and turn to mud. Landing them on the same steps is most of what makes a beat sound finished. The melody is written an octave above the bass for the same reason — so the two aren't in each other's way.

BUILD also sets a scale if you haven't chosen one. The default "16-pad classic" layout is the original hardware's fixed note order and isn't a scale at all — the pads don't even run in pitch order — so a melody written across it comes out closer to noise than a tune. If you've picked your own key, BUILD leaves it alone.

Recording the effects

The effects have their own lane now, the same as the keyboard. In the fx tab press rec (or arm the device's LIVE REC, which does both), press PLAY, and hold an effect — every step the playhead crosses while you hold it is written down. Let go and it stops recording there, so a two-bar reverse comes back as a two-bar reverse rather than a single blip.

On playback the lane engages and releases the effect for you. Touching a pad always overrides it, so you can play over your own recording without the two fighting. clear wipes the lane on this pattern.

Drum kits

The keys tab has a drum kit row: 808, 909 and lo-fi. These are built out of maths rather than loaded from files, so they add nothing to the download, and they load onto the selected drum slot's 16 pads as real audio — meaning you can trim them, chop them, lock them per step and everything else, exactly like a sample. Kick, snare, hats, clap, toms, rim and cowbell, in that order across the pads.

Projects

The PROJ button on the screen opens the project browser. A project is a complete snapshot: patterns, the chain, tempo, swing, volume, which samples are in which slots, and the keyboard track and the effects lane. Those last two used to be left out — you could save a beat with a synth part in it, reopen it, and the synth part would be missing. Anything the sequencer can play is saved now.

  • + new starts a genuinely empty project — every pattern cleared, not the shipped demo beats.
  • save as keeps a new copy under the name in the box; save updates the one you're in.
  • Tap a project to select it. Its actions appear underneath: open, rename, move to a folder, copy, export, delete.
  • Folders group your projects — the chips along the top filter by folder, and + folder makes a new one.
  • export writes a real .po33.json file to your downloads, and import reads one back — so a beat can move between browsers, devices, or to a friend. export all backs up everything at once.

Projects live in the browser's own storage, which survives site updates. Exporting is how you get them off this device.

Tap the little guy

Tap him where he stands on the screen and he climbs out of it. Once he's loose he wanders the actual interface — the real edges of the pads, the mode row, the sliders and the perform tabs are the ledges he stands on, so he's walking on the instrument rather than on a picture of one.

He takes his time. He strolls, stops to look around, turns at the edge of a pad rather than marching off it (though he misjudges it now and then, and falls), and occasionally goes to stand under a blinking red LED to watch it. Press a pad and he looks over; press one near him and he might wander across to see what it was.

Press PLAY and he dances — turns to face you and bounces on every step, harder on the downbeat, with a little side-step each half bar. He keeps it up while the sequencer runs.

He can never get in your way: the layer he's drawn on ignores taps entirely, so every pad underneath him still works. Tap him again and he goes home; he also lets himself back in after a few minutes, and he never survives a reload. He touches nothing — no audio, no patterns, nothing saved.

The MIX tab

Mute and solo, plus the pattern chain, on one screen — the "what is my arrangement actually doing" view.

  • Slots with nothing on them in this pattern are dimmed, so you can see what you are actually muting.
  • The chain row shows your arrangement, with the currently playing link lit. Tap a link to remove it — no more starting the whole chain over because of one wrong tap.
  • The add row appends a pattern to the end.
  • Every chain edit is a separate undo step.

The song view

Tap song in the top bar of the screen. Every sound that has something on it gets a row of sixteen cells, filled where it hits and hollow where it rests — the whole pattern at a glance. The outlined column is where the playhead is. Along the top: which pattern you are on, the tempo, the scale, how many parts are playing, how many hits there are in total, how full the bar is as a percentage, how many steps have parameter locks, anything muted or soloed, and how long a bar and a full chain last in seconds. Each row also names the sample loaded in that slot. Muted parts are drawn hollow; a soloed one is underlined. Along the bottom: your pattern chain, with the one you are playing highlighted. Tap scene to go back to the animation.

The 16 punch-in effects

Same set, same order as the hardware. Hold FX + a pad, or use the fx tab in the performance strip under the screen. They are momentary: they last exactly as long as you hold, and they never change your pattern — so you can lean on them while recording the master out.

Most of them are not audio effects at all. They bend the sequencer: they shorten the loop, reverse the step pointer or stutter it, and they pin the pattern chain so the bar repeats instead of moving on.

  1. loop 16 — loops the whole 16-step bar and stops the chain moving on
  2. loop 12 — loops the first 12 steps, so the bar limps and everything shifts
  3. loop short — loops 4 steps around where you are
  4. loop shorter — loops 2 steps, near machine-gun
  5. unison — thickens the whole beat into one wide layer
  6. unison low — unison with the low end pushed, good under drums
  7. octave up — whole beat an octave up
  8. octave down — whole beat an octave down
  9. stutter 4 — holds each step across a group of 4
  10. stutter 3 — holds each step across a group of 3, three-against-four
  11. scratch — pitch-bends the held step like a hand on a record
  12. scratch fast — the same scratch at twice the speed
  13. 6/8 quantize — forces a triplet shuffle onto the grid
  14. retrigger — re-fires the pattern from step 1 every 4 steps
  15. reversed — plays the bar backwards
  16. no effect — bypass. The safe pad: it kills whatever was running
